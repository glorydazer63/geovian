### Email Communications
Email has been around for decades, and despite what people say in defence of it, Email is definitely showing its age and with many of its (lack of) security features still very much evident, it should really be put out to pasture. Netizens are still blind to the convenience of email, perhaps not realising the inherent dangers (past and present) which lurk in mailboxes such as executable viruses, trojan horses, a myriad of spam attacks, as well as phishing attempts.

Email has proven itself time and again to be a continuing threat to all sizes of business. It has one absolute critical flaw, and that is: **Plain Text Mail Headers**. These headers can not be hidden, obfuscated or encrypted, simply because the email will have no idea where to land on the Internet without it. This flaw has been existent since it first started to be commercially available back in the early 80's, and Big Tech loves it because email as a whole is unfettered.

Don't let Email companies who provide so-called encrypted email fool you. In the main, that is totally a myth. The only encryption occurs in two instances:

  - Where email is sent and received on the same domain (like users on the google.com domain).
  - where email is in transit across interconnected domains.

But email sent by you to your email provider is not encrypted, nor is it encrypted at the far end of the chain as email leaves the receivers email provider and sends it on to them. Purely because Plain Text Mail Headers are required to send to, and then receive from. Without it, your email doesn't work. Remember this when dealing with the likes of Proton Mail or Tuta Mail.

The other issue is that users in general are too lazy and too dumb to do anything about it anyway. No wonder Big Tech keeps advocating for the Golden Goose. 
 
Some mail providers are better than others, but once you know the real truth, you'll look at every Email Provider in a different light. The only way to fix it is to start using encrypted Messenger apps. Onsite email systems managed by in-house staff are particularly vulnerable due to gaps in staff technical knowledge and in some cases insufficient resources to manage an external attack of magnitude.

Much better is for your mail to be managed externally if your business policy allows for it. With small business and sole traders this is probably less of a concern but it still pays to keep your wits about you. Uncertainty can strike at any time. Also, bear in mind what I wrote about mail encryption as above.

The following is an overview of some of the well known mail providers out there. The comments given are the opinion of the site author only.

**Gmail** - you can still keep a Gmail account if you must (particularly for using an Android phone because there's no way around that, especially if you want to download and pay for apps) but you don't have to use it for correspondence and communication. We would recommend not using Gmail for email purposes EVER! It is known in the tech trade as the 'great mail harvester'. Google will tell you there is privacy built in. Sure, for everybody else except them. Avoid!

**Hotmail, Live, Outlook** - should be called 'Notmail'. For your own peace of mind, use another mail provider. Not this POS.

**Yahoo** - if there is anyone still using this crappy mail system, get off it, get off now! 

**Protonmail and Tutanota** - two privacy respecting services, one out of Switzerland, the other from Germany. I've used them on/off over the years but because they are both encrypted systems.. apparently, they can't be used to download into a mail aggregator, like Thunderbird, K9 Mail or Mailspring for instance. Good for what they are, and even on their own, they present slightly better choices than Gmail, even if they advocate with  encryption which in all honesty is a load of hogwash. Both have free and paid accounts. Take your pick.

https://ProtonMail.com\\
https://Tutanota.com

**GMX** - an email provider with a large user base mostly across Europe which like some other email providers I use, but I'm fairly ignorant as to whether my privacy is fully respected, but because they're a German provider, you do have EU protections in place which does provide a level of comfort I guess. I've tried to access my GMX mail via an external email aggregator but I wasn't able to get it to work due to encryption no doubt. It's free (as well as a paid version) and works well on desktop and mobile.

Refer: https://gmx.com

**Mail.com** - it's free (obviously there's a paid plan too), and it comes with a whole bunch of cool features which might appeal to some of the readers here. You have the ability to set up a mailbox on any one of Mail.com's sub-domains, and there are a few of them. Mostly this is a good choice but like GMX above there's no ability to aggregate your mailbox from the system to another offsite solution like Thunderbird. 

Refer: https://mail.com

**Mailbox.org** - Mailbox is a family run email business out of Germany which I've been using since 2023. I use it for Email alias, it's my CALDAV and CARDAV Server central access point for all my devices, plus it has an encrypted Drive space which I use to upload my critical encrypted files to Mailbox's offsite location from my devices and my residence, just in case my phone or residence is compromised. more about Securing Offsite Backups in another to come.

Refer: https://mailbox.org

**Riseup** - for many years I've wanted to use this platform, but you need to be invited onto it. Because I knew no one that was using it, I couldn't get on. It does have a good privacy reputation, and for those that are successful in getting on, please let us know how it went.

Refer: https://riseup.org

**Disroot** - I've recently signed on with this lot, based on three factors:

  - it's free.
  - it can be aggregated into an all-in-one mail aggregator.
  - has very good security and privacy provisions. 
  
However, I had problems getting past the account validation. There is a proviso, in that your account is vetted and approved within 48 hours of you signing up. Good luck to those that are successful.

Refer: https://disroot.org

**Your own domain and private email** - this is my preferred method of email, where you control the email configuration 100%. Creating your own domain isn't difficult to set up, and once done, setting the email up is even easier. Also, nearly 99.9% of the time, any mailboxes created under your domain can be aggregated into an all-in-one mailbox aggregator whether using something like Thunderbird on the desktop or any number of aggregator apps on Android (like BlueMail, TypeApp or K9) or iOS.
